How Much Should Freelancers Set Aside for Taxes? A Simple System for 2026

By XVisionary · Updated Oct 13, 2026 · About 4 min read

Free tax set-aside calculator: get a rough % to save from each payment.

If you're wondering how much to set aside for taxes as a freelancer, a common starting point is 25% to 30% of every payment, moved into a separate savings account the day you're paid. Below is a simple system: pick a percentage, separate the money, know the 2026 IRS due dates, and track it in one place.

Nobody withholds tax from freelance or side-hustle payments. That money is still owed; it's just sitting in your checking account looking spendable. The fix isn't a fancy app. It's a habit: every time a client pays you, move a fixed slice of it somewhere you won't touch.

This guide uses US federal rules and 2026 figures. If you're outside the US, the habit still applies, but the numbers won't.

Step 1: Pick your freelance tax set-aside percentage

Many freelancers start by saving somewhere between 25% and 30% of every payment, then adjust once they've filed a year or talked to a tax pro. Higher earners and people in high-tax states often need more. The exact number matters less than doing it on every single payment.

Want a number based on your own income and expenses? The free tax set-aside calculator gives a rough percentage in about 30 seconds.

Why freelancers need to save so much: self-employment tax

Self-employed people pay self-employment tax on top of income tax. Per the IRS, that's 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare), applied to 92.35% of your net profit. It kicks in once your net earnings from self-employment hit $400. For 2026, the Social Security part stops at $184,500 of combined earnings.

Quick example (made-up numbers): $8,000 of net profit means about $8,000 × 0.9235 × 0.153 ≈ $1,130 in self-employment tax alone, before any federal or state income tax.

Step 2: Open a separate tax savings account

Not a mental note. A real, separate account, ideally at a different bank than your checking so it's slightly annoying to raid.

Step 3: Move the money the day you get paid

Payment lands, you move your percentage. If a client pays $850 and you save 25%, $212.50 goes to the tax account right away.

Step 4: Know the 2026 quarterly estimated tax due dates

The IRS splits the year into uneven chunks (from Form 1040-ES):

Income earnedFederal payment due
Jan 1 – Mar 31, 2026April 15, 2026
Apr 1 – May 31, 2026June 15, 2026
Jun 1 – Aug 31, 2026September 15, 2026
Sep 1 – Dec 31, 2026January 15, 2027

Yes, the second "quarter" is only two months. That trips a lot of people up. Your state may have its own estimated-tax schedule, so check that too.

Step 5: Track your set-aside in one place

Log each payment with the date, the amount, the set-aside, and whether you've actually moved it. At each due date, you'll know exactly what you've saved for that quarter. A plain spreadsheet with those four columns is enough.

Freelance tax set-aside FAQ

How much should I set aside for taxes as a freelancer?

Many freelancers start with 25% to 30% of each payment and adjust after their first year of filing or a conversation with a tax professional. If you earn more or live in a high-tax state, you may need more. The habit of saving on every payment matters more than getting the exact percentage right on day one.

Do freelancers have to pay quarterly estimated taxes?

Often, yes. The IRS generally expects estimated payments if you expect to owe $1,000 or more in federal tax for the year after withholding and credits. Freelancers and side hustlers with no withholding usually hit that quickly. Check IRS Form 1040-ES or a tax professional for your situation.

When is the next estimated tax payment due?

For income earned September 1 through December 31, 2026, the federal estimated payment is due January 15, 2027. The other 2026 dates were April 15, June 15, and September 15, 2026.

Is self-employment tax on top of income tax?

Yes. Self-employment tax (15.3% on 92.35% of net profit, per the IRS) covers Social Security and Medicare. Regular federal income tax, and any state income tax, comes on top of that. That's why a freelance set-aside percentage tends to be higher than people expect.

What happens if I miss a quarterly estimated tax payment?

The IRS can charge an underpayment penalty, which works like interest on the amount that was late. Paying as soon as you can usually keeps it smaller. A tax professional can tell you whether a "safe harbor" rule applies to you.

The short version

  1. Pick a percentage (start around 25–30%, confirm with a pro).
  2. Open a separate account.
  3. Move it the day you're paid.
  4. Pay on the four dates.
  5. Track it.
